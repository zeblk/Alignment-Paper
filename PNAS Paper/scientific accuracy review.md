**Scientific accuracy review — Living Alignment, medium edits**

Reviewed September 21, 2026. This review covers the active manuscript, including every substantive section; commented-out draft material is excluded. The standard is substantial factual mistakes and clear misrepresentations of cited sources. Interpretive extensions, philosophical disagreements, and missing fine qualifications are not treated as errors.

I found no major scientific misrepresentation in the claims that could be verified. There is one narrow but definite technical wording error, plus three incorrect bibliographic records. These findings do not overturn the examples or the paper's argument. No manuscript or bibliography changes were made during this review.

**Follow-up:** The Brown, CIRL, and Off-Switch bibliography findings below have since been corrected. See the [complete citation audit](citation%20audit.md) for verification of all 137 cited references. This document records the earlier scientific review.

**Corrections worth making**

1. **Predictive coding: a prediction residual is not necessarily orthogonal to the prediction.** [Manuscript line 292](</Users/zeb/Documents/GitHub/Alignment-Paper/PNAS Paper/pnas_paper medium edits.tex:292>) says that propagated activity is “specifically what is orthogonal to predictions.” Rao and Ballard describe differences between actual and predicted activity. Their model does not generally enforce orthogonality. This is a narrow technical error rather than a failure of the surrounding interpretation. Suggested replacement: “so ascending activity carries the residual difference between predicted and actual activity.” Checked against the abstract, Figure 1, and Methods equations 3–7 in the [original paper](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rao/Rao-Ballard-NN-1999.pdf).

2. **The Brown (2003) reference is incorrect.** [Bibliography entry `brown2003rhythmic`](</Users/zeb/Documents/GitHub/Alignment-Paper/assets/proxyfailure.bib:2414>), cited at manuscript line 296, gives “A rhythmic mechanism for communication in the cortex,” *Trends in Neurosciences* 26(5):232–233. Those pages overlap an unrelated article by Lucia Notterpek, [“Neurotrophins in myelination: a new role for a puzzling receptor”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12744836/). A real, relevant alternative is Peter Brown, [“Oscillatory nature of human basal ganglia activity: relationship to the pathophysiology of Parkinson's disease”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12671940/), *Movement Disorders* 18(4):357–363 (2003), DOI 10.1002/mds.10358. That is a proposed replacement, not a claim about which source was originally intended. Alternatively, remove the Brown citation: [Hammond et al. (2007)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17532060/) already supports the substantive Parkinson's claim.

3. **CIRL has the wrong third author.** [Entry `hadfieldmenell2016cooperative`](</Users/zeb/Documents/GitHub/Alignment-Paper/assets/proxyfailure.bib:3793>) lists “Fisac, Pieter.” The third author is **Pieter Abbeel**. The complete author list is Dylan Hadfield-Menell, Anca Dragan, Pieter Abbeel, and Stuart Russell. [Original proceedings record](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2016/hash/c3395dd46c34fa7fd8d729d8cf88b7a8-Abstract.html).

4. **The Off-Switch Game has an omitted author and the wrong venue.** [Entry `hadfieldmenell2017off`](</Users/zeb/Documents/GitHub/Alignment-Paper/assets/proxyfailure.bib:3802>) omits **Pieter Abbeel**. It appeared at **IJCAI 2017**, not AAAI 2017. The existing pages, 220–227, are correct; the DOI is 10.24963/ijcai.2017/32. [Original proceedings record](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/32).

**Verification limits**

The review checked key empirical examples, technical mechanisms, and potentially consequential attributions against original articles, targeted book passages, author copies, publisher records, or authoritative abstracts. It was not a cover-to-cover rereading of every book or a full audit of all 137 references.

Three concrete gaps remain: the exact steel-punch/coin origin detail in line 216 was not established from Johnson's Gutenberg passage; Palmer's original 1954 study was not retrieved independently of Zollman's account; and the precise Holland/Jaderberg support for agent subpopulations in line 429 was not established at page level. These are verification limits, not demonstrated errors. Some publisher pages were inaccessible; alternate author copies and abstracts were used where available.

Fine distinctions such as performance metrics in Bernstein's experiment, variability in OCD insight, alternative measures of neural criticality, or whether reward uncertainty entails technical decision-theoretic risk aversion have deliberately not been presented as errors. They fall below the requested threshold.
